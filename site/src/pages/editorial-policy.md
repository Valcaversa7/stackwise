This page exists so you can hold us to something specific. It covers how we write, how we test, how we correct, and where money could influence us.

## 1. Accuracy

Every factual claim in an article is either tested by us, linked to a primary source, or clearly marked as our opinion. Commands and configuration examples are run before publication, on the version stated in the article.

When a tool's behaviour changes after publication, we update the article and change the "updated" date. We do not silently rewrite; substantive corrections carry a note.

## 2. Corrections

We correct mistakes. The process:

1. Email [stackwiseorg@gmail.com](mailto:stackwiseorg@gmail.com) with the article URL and what you believe is wrong.
2. We verify. If you are right, we fix it.
3. Minor fixes (typos, broken links) are made quietly. Substantive fixes — a wrong number, a mistaken claim, a change of verdict — get a dated correction note at the bottom of the article.

We do not delete articles we have come to disagree with. We add a note and move on.

## 3. Reviews and testing

- **Method is published.** If we measured it, we say how.
- **Versions are stated.** Software versions, firmware versions, OS versions.
- **Negative results are published.** If a product fails a test, that is the finding.
- **We buy what we can.** Purchased units are treated as more reliable evidence than loaned ones, because there is no relationship to protect.

## 4. Review units and press samples

If a manufacturer sends us hardware or grants us software access, the article says so in the first paragraph. We do not agree to return conditions that affect the verdict, and we do not sign non-disparagement clauses.

A manufacturer may be given the opportunity to correct **factual** errors about their product before publication. They do not see the assessment, the verdict or the prose, and they cannot request changes to any of it.

## 5. Advertising and affiliate relationships

- Advertisers have no advance sight of editorial content and no ability to influence it.
- Affiliate links are labelled where they appear.
- We do not accept payment for coverage or for a positive assessment.
- Commission rates do not affect rankings or recommendations.
- We remove affiliate links from products we stop recommending.

See the [Affiliate Disclosure](/affiliate-disclosure/) for the programmes we participate in.

## 6. Sponsored content

We do not publish sponsored posts, advertorials or paid placements in the editorial stream. If that ever changes, sponsored content will be labelled in the header of the article, in a style you cannot miss, and it will be excluded from the newsletter.

## 7. AI-generated content

We use AI tools for research assistance, drafting support and code explanation. We do not publish AI-generated articles. Every article on this site is written, tested and edited by a named human who takes responsibility for it, and every byline is a real person.

Where an AI tool contributed materially to a specific section, we say so in that section.

## 8. Conflicts of interest

If a writer has a financial or personal interest in a subject — employment, shareholding, a personal relationship with someone at the company — it is disclosed in the article. Where the conflict is significant, a different writer covers it.

## 9. Right of reply

If an article is critical of a company or product, we will offer that company a chance to respond before publication where we have reasonable contact details and time allows. Substantive responses are published with the article.

## 10. Complaints

Complaints about content go to [stackwiseorg@gmail.com](mailto:stackwiseorg@gmail.com). Complaints about advertising or privacy go to [stackwiseorg@gmail.com](mailto:stackwiseorg@gmail.com). We acknowledge within three working days and resolve within thirty.
